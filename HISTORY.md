# A short history of the MacBook8,1

Apple announced the MacBook (Retina, 12-inch, Early 2015) on March 9, 2015, and started shipping it on April 10. The identifier is `MacBook8,1`. The case is model A1534, EMC 2746. It stayed on sale until April 19, 2016, when the 2016 refresh replaced it. One year, give or take a week. In that year you could buy a laptop that weighed 2.03 pounds, tapered from 0.52 inch down to 0.14, and had one port.

That port was USB-C. Not Thunderbolt. The other hole was a headphone jack, which felt like a mercy. Power, an external display, and the spare keyboard you needed if the built-in one was dead all had to share the same oval. It came in silver, gold, and space gray. Rose gold waited for the next model. The $1,299 one was a 1.1 GHz Core M-5Y31 with 256 GB. The $1,599 one was a 1.2 GHz M-5Y51 with 512 GB. A build-to-order 1.3 GHz M-5Y71 could take either disk. Every one of them had 8 GB of RAM soldered to the board, Intel HD Graphics 5300, and a 12-inch 2304x1440 display. None of them had a fan. The chip was a Broadwell Core M, a few watts, the sort of part Intel had aimed at tablets and then asked to run OS X without making a sound.

Apple never published how many it built. The public numbers are other people's. In March 2015, before one had shipped, Ming-Chi Kuo at KGI forecast 450,000 for the first half of the year. That half was really the spring quarter. Around the same time, reports had Quanta already holding about 160,000 assembled machines, waiting on screens, with LG expected to supply on the order of 120,000 panels a month once production got going. Digitimes guessed the 12-inch would be 15 to 20 percent of Apple's notebook shipments for 2015. A year of the Kuo pace would land under a million. That is a doodle, not a shipment report. Do not quote it as if Apple had counted.

The butterfly keyboard showed up here first. Short travel, a mechanism that looked annoyed by fingernails. Later MacBook Pros made it famous for failing. This one just introduced it. The Force Touch trackpad arrived the same week on the 13-inch Pro, so that one is a shared first. The FaceTime camera was 480p, which was already a joke in 2015 and has not become funnier. The battery was a stack of terraced cells, 39.7 watt-hours, glued into the bottom case so the laptop could be that thin. iFixit opened one on April 15, 2015. Pentalobe screws on the bottom, tri-wing screws on the only port, a logic board you had to lift before the battery would unplug, and a battery glued down including the center cell. Processor, RAM, and flash were soldered. The display glass was fused to the panel. They scored it 1 out of 10 and wrote that Apple had nearly managed a zero.

Linux took this personally. The keyboard and trackpad sit on an SPI bus whose DMA engine accepts a transfer and then never finishes it, so a stock boot times out until something forces the controller into PIO. Deep sleep can wake the machine and leave that bus dead until the next reboot. The speakers are a CS4208 that a stock driver can see and not usefully play. The camera is not a USB webcam. It wants a driver from outside the kernel and a firmware blob pulled out of an old macOS update. And because there is one port, the keyboard you plug in to type a disk password is also the charger. Apple stopped new macOS releases at Big Sur. The laptop is still pretty. It would just like to type.
